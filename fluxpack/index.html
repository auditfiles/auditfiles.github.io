Fluxpack

The induction backpack. We're building a pack that makes electricity as you walk: a magnet swings through a coil with every step, and a small circuit stores the current in a battery.

Made by Aadit Naik (UC Berkeley ’30) and Kaviya Ramesh (Georgia Tech ’30)

Simplified diagram, not to scale

What it is

Fluxpack is a backpack with a small generator built in. As you walk, a magnet inside swings back and forth through a coil of wire. That motion induces an electric current, and a short chain of circuit modules turns it into steady power for a lithium-ion battery.

So far we have built and tested the three main parts on their own: the coil, the power circuit and the pendulum. Next, they go into a backpack.

Why it matters

People who deal with frequent power outages are often left unable to keep small essentials charged. A pack that makes its own power as you walk doesn't need sunlight or an outlet. Hikers and others far from any outlet face a similar problem.

How it works

  1. Walk

    The body does the work

    Each step shakes the pack. A magnet inside slides back and forth through the coil.

  2. Induce

    A moving field makes current

    The changing magnetic flux through the coil induces an alternating voltage across its ends.

  3. Store

    Power circuit to battery

    Three small modules turn that AC into steady DC, step it up, and charge a lithium-ion cell.

Faraday's law of induction
\[ \varepsilon = -N\,\frac{d\Phi}{dt} \]

ε = −N · dΦ/dt

More turns N and a faster-changing flux Φ give more voltage, which is why the coil has 5,000 turns.

The power chain

Stage 1 LTC3588 AC in → regulated DC out

Rectifies the coil's alternating output into steady DC.

Stage 2 MT3608 DC in → higher DC out

Boost converter that steps the voltage up.

Stage 3 TP4056 DC in → Li-ion cell

Charging module for the lithium-ion battery.
